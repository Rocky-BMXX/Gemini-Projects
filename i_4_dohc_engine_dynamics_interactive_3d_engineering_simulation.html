Inline-4 Engine Engineering Manual

1. The 180° Flat-Plane Crankshaft Architecture

In a traditional automotive inline-four engine, the four crank throws are oriented in a single flat plane (180 degrees apart). Cylinders 1 and 4 move together as an outer pair, while cylinders 2 and 3 move together as an inner pair 180° out of phase. This cancels out primary reciprocating forces ($F_{primary} = m r \omega^2 \cos\theta$), but introduces a secondary unbalance at twice engine speed ($2\omega$).

2. Firing Sequence (1 - 3 - 4 - 2)

Because a complete four-stroke cycle requires two full crankshaft revolutions (720°), a power stroke occurs every 180° of crankshaft rotation. The firing sequence 1-3-4-2 provides even combustion spacing along the block, minimizing torsional twisting of the crankshaft.

Stroke 1: Intake (0° - 180°) Piston descends from TDC to BDC. Intake cam opens intake valves. Fresh air and fuel are drawn into the cylinder bore.
Stroke 2: Compression (180° - 360°) Both valves close completely. Piston ascends from BDC to TDC, compressing the trapped charge into the high-compression combustion dome.
Stroke 3: Combustion / Power (360° - 540°) Spark plug creates an intense plasma arc just before TDC. Rapid thermal expansion creates up to 80 bar of pressure, driving the piston down to produce engine torque.
Stroke 4: Exhaust (540° - 720°) Exhaust cam opens exhaust valves. Piston sweeps upward, expelling burnt gases into the tuned exhaust headers.
